Designing a frame with nothing to hide

Forty-one sketches, nine prototypes and one rule: if you can see a cable, a bolt or a weld, we haven't finished.

By Mateo Ruiz, Industrial Designer

41sketches

Design

Every bike we admired had the same quality: you could understand it at a glance. The tubes went where the forces went. Nothing was decoration. We set ourselves one rule for the Volta One frame — if you can see a cable, a bolt or a weld, we haven't finished.

Forty-one sketches

The first sketches were wild: a monocoque shell, a split top tube, a frame that hid the battery under a cover. Each one solved a problem by adding something. By sketch 30 we had learned to solve problems by taking things away. Sketch 41 is the bike you see: one continuous line from head tube to rear axle.

Hiding the battery in plain sight

A 720 Wh battery is the size of a wine bottle. Hiding it usually means a fat down tube that makes the bike look swollen. We hydroformed the aluminium into a slightly flattened oval, 4 mm wider than a conventional tube, so the battery disappears without the frame changing character.

Smoothing every weld

Each frame is TIG-welded and then hand-finished for 40 minutes until the joints flow into the tubes. It's slow and it's expensive. It's also the difference between a product and an object.

"We stopped asking: what can we add? We started asking: what is this part for?"

Nine prototypes later

Prototype nine survived 100,000 cycles on the fatigue rig and 3,000 km of Amsterdam cobblestones. It is also, we think, the calmest-looking e-bike we've ever seen. That was the point.
